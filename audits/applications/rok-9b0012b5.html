## 1. Executive Summary & Value Proposition

Arango's thesis — that AI agents need *unified, current, trusted business context* rather than a patchwork of a vector DB, a graph DB, a document store, and a search index — is fundamentally an **operational** thesis, not only a data-model thesis. Native multimodel convergence means one process must simultaneously satisfy three workloads with violently different resource profiles: nested document CRUD (IOPS + write amplification), deep graph traversal (pointer-chasing, memory-resident edge caches, cross-shard coordination), and ANN/BM25 retrieval (SIMD-heavy, memory-bandwidth bound). Every one of those must be schedulable, upgradeable, and observable **on Kubernetes** without an operator babysitting it at 03:00.

That intersection — Go control-plane engineering against a stateful, consensus-backed, multimodel database — is precisely where my background sits.

**Direct alignment with the problem space:**

| Go services at scale | Idiomatic Go: `context`-first APIs, `errgroup`-bounded fan-out, `singleflight` cache collapse, zero-allocation hot paths, escape-analysis-driven struct design, `pprof`/`trace` flamegraph triage |

The strategic value I bring is not "another Go engineer." It is an architect who can **close the loop from CRD to production**, and who treats the operator itself as a product with an SLO — because every minute of operator-induced downtime is a minute of eroded trust in the context layer that AI agents are supposed to reason over.
