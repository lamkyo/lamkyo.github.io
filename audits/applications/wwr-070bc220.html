## 1. Executive Summary & Value Proposition

### Why this problem is a natural fit

The Steuart Nutrition role is deceptively hard to fill. It reads like "customize an LMS," but the actual job is narrower and more interesting: **build a training system that produces defensible compliance evidence inside a GMP-certified dietary-supplement contract manufacturer, while shipping fast enough that plant and office teams don't route around it.**

Those two forces pull in opposite directions:

| Force | What it demands |
|---|---|
| GMP / 21 CFR Part 111 | Immutable, attributable, time-stamped training records; qualified-personnel evidence; re-training triggered by deviations and CAPAs |
| "Extreme Ownership, fast turnaround" | Low-ceremony iteration; solutions shipped in days, not quarters; onboarding of new customers (and their SKUs) without engineering bottlenecks |

Most candidates optimize for one and quietly break the other. A vanilla LMS implementation gives you screens, not auditability. A heavyweight QMS integration gives you auditability and a two-week change-request cycle. The value I bring is **an architecture that makes the compliance artifact a byproduct of the workflow rather than a separate process** — so the plant supervisor's 30-second action is simultaneously the training event, the qualification record, and the audit-trail entry.

### Direct alignment with the stack and domain

- **AI-assisted development as a discipline, not a novelty.** The posting lists Claude, Cursor, Copilot, and Grok. I treat these as a heterogeneous toolchain with different failure modes, and I have a working method for each: spec-first prompting, generated-code review checklists, test-harness-first scaffolding, and explicit verification gates. More on this in §2.4 — it is the single most load-bearing skill in this role and most applicants hand-wave it.
- **Fast platform absorption.** The job requires learning a third-party platform's codebase well enough to extend it safely. My approach is mechanical and repeatable: map the data model and the extension seams before touching a line of code, then write a throwaway spike proving the seam holds. That process is spelled out in §2.2.
- **Regulated-manufacturing literacy.** I understand why a contract manufacturer cares about electronic records and signatures, why "the tablet lost Wi-Fi mid-module" is a compliance incident and not a bug report, and why batch-record and change-control systems are the integrations that actually matter. That is what turns a generic LMS hire into someone who ships the right thing first.
- **APIs, integrations, web apps.** Primary stack: TypeScript (Node.js, Fastify/NestJS, React) and Python (FastAPI). PostgreSQL as the system of record. Comfortable owning a service end-to-end — schema, API, background jobs, deploy, dashboards.

**What I'd deliver in the first 90 days:** a documented extension map of the training platform, a Part 11–grade training-record service running alongside it, one high-value automation shipped to the plant floor, and a CI/CD + observability baseline so the next feature doesn't cost more than this one.

**Design stance:** the LMS stays the *delivery* system (content, quizzes, UX). We own the *record of truth* about who was qualified, on what, when, and under which content version. That separation is what lets you swap or re-skin the platform without rewriting your compliance history — and it's the piece a generic LMS customization always gets wrong.
