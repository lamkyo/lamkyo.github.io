Executive Hook — What You Actually Need (And What Most Bidders Will Miss) You asked for a platform that "watches Binance, Coinbase, Kraken and more simultaneously." That sentence hides the entire difficulty of this project. The hard part is **not** connecting to exchanges — every bidder will show you a WebSocket demo that ticks a price. The hard part is the three problems nobody puts in their proposal: **Pitfall #1 — The "and more" trap.** A naive build hardcodes three exchange integrations. The moment you want a fourth, you pay for a rewrite. The correct architecture is an **exchange adapter abstraction**: a single normalized internal schema (order book, trades, volume, open interest) that every venue maps into. Adding an exchange becomes a config file, not a sprint. **Pitfall #2 — Order-book integrity.** Live order books are not "a list of prices." They are a **sequenced delta stream** that must be reconstructed against a REST snapshot with strict sequence-gap detection. Miss a sequence number and your book silently corrupts — you'll see phantom liquidity and make decisions on data that never existed. Most bidders ship a UI that *looks* live but is quietly wrong. We treat gap detection and resync as a first-class feature, not an afterthought. **Pitfall #3 — Rate limits, reconnects, and clock skew.** Exchanges throttle, disconnect, and drift. A production monitor needs exponential backoff, heartbeat monitoring, and per-venue rate-limit budgeting — otherwise your dashboard freezes at the exact moment volatility spikes, which is the only moment you care about. **The real deliverable is trust.** You are not buying a dashboard. You are buying a system whose numbers you can act on. Every architectural decision below serves that single goal. --- ## 2.
