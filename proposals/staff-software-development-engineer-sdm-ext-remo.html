1. Executive Hook — The Diagnostic

Delinea's brief reads like a platform summary, but the *actual* engineering need underneath it is sharper than it looks. You are not hiring for "identity security" in the abstract — you are hiring a Staff-level SDM (Software Development Manager / Senior Development Engineer) who can operate at the intersection of three hard constraints that most bidders will gloss over:

The three pitfalls most bidders miss:

1. Context propagation across the identity lifecycle is the real product. Delinea's differentiator is *context applied throughout the entire identity lifecycle* — cloud, traditional infra, data, SaaS, and AI. The subtle trap is that "context" is not a feature; it is a cross-cutting concern that must be threaded through every authorization decision. Any implementation that treats context as a per-service concern will fragment and fail at scale.

2. "Centralized authorization" + "AI-powered intelligence" is a latency budget problem. Centralized decisioning means every access request round-trips to a policy engine. If the AI layer is synchronous and inline, you inherit its p99 latency on every authz call. The correct architecture separates decisioning (fast, deterministic) from intelligence (async, advisory) — a distinction that determines whether the platform is usable or not.

3. Machine identities outnumber human identities by orders of magnitude. A solution tuned for human SSO flows will collapse under machine-identity volume. The SDM must design for non-interactive, high-throughput, short-lived credential patterns from day one — not retrofit them.

What this proposal delivers: a scoped, verifiable, regression-safe engineering increment that demonstrates exactly this architectural judgment — delivered in 3–4 days at a fixed $250, with full handover.

4. Regression Guardrails — Zero-Regression Commitment

Regression safety is not a promise; it is a process. Here is exactly how it is enforced:

1. Baseline-first. Phase 1 captures the pre-change behavior and metrics. Nothing is "fixed" without a before/after comparison. 2. Characterization tests. Before modifying any existing module, I write tests that pin its *current* behavior — so any accidental change fails loudly. 3. Additive-by-default. New behavior is added behind interfaces/flags; existing call sites are not silently rewritten. 4. Full suite gate. The entire existing test suite must pass, unmodified, before handover. 5. Contract locking. Public interfaces are frozen with contract tests; breaking changes require an explicit, documented decision. 6. Fail-closed authz. On the authorization path, errors deny by default — never allow. 7. Reversible delivery. Every change lands on a branch with a documented rollback path. You merge; I don't. 8. Diff discipline. Small, reviewable commits — no "big bang" merges that hide regressions.

Net guarantee: if the existing suite passes before and after, and contracts are locked, regressions cannot ship silently.
