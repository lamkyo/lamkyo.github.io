1. Executive Hook — The Diagnostic

You've asked for "a Shopify store integrated with my existing website." That single sentence hides the three decisions that actually determine whether this project succeeds or quietly bleeds money six months from now:

Pitfall #1 — "Integration" is ambiguous, and the wrong interpretation is expensive. There are two fundamentally different architectures hiding behind the word *integrate*: - Subdomain handoff (`shop.yourdomain.com` → Shopify-hosted storefront), or - Headless / embedded commerce (Shopify as the backend, your existing site as the presentation layer via Storefront API).

Most bidders will silently pick the cheaper option (subdomain) and bill you for "integration." If your existing site is your brand's primary traffic and SEO surface, that choice fragments your domain authority and your analytics. We will make this decision explicitly, in writing, on Day 1 — before a single line of theme code is written.

Pitfall #2 — Custom features are where fixed-price projects die. "Implement custom..." (truncated in your brief) is the highest-risk phrase in the posting. Custom functionality scoped loosely becomes unbounded. We counter this with a written feature contract: every custom requirement is enumerated, estimated, and either included or explicitly deferred — no ambiguity at handover.

Pitfall #3 — Nobody tests the checkout. Themes get built, the homepage looks great, and then a discount code, a tax rule, or a shipping zone silently breaks conversion. We ship with an automated verification pass covering cart, checkout, and integration endpoints — not just a visual "looks good to me."

What you actually need: a scoped, tested Shopify storefront that either cleanly hands off from or embeds into your existing site, with custom features frozen in a written contract, and a handover package you own outright.
